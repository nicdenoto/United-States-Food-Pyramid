Introduction and Background: There has been large debate and discussion ,within the field of Epidemiology and public health policy and decision making, surrounding the proper structuring of an "evidence hiearchy".  When establishing claims and recommendations for public health, it is important that organizations and governments involved in public health policy are relying on strong evidence structures. In contrast to some fields of research, the promotion of recommendations, especially on such a large, public scale, can be immediately damaging to individuals health if the recommendations are harmful; accidental harmful recommendations in health can result from relying on weak evidence structures to establish causal claims for human health.  The supplementation of HRT for women has wavered between a positive and negative intervention as new research has emerged throughout the years.  In the 1970's, clinical trials linking HRT to an icnread cancer risk caused the dismantling of doctoral promotion for HRT, until the addition of progesterone was scene to minimize this proposed risk. A study in 2002, again, linked HRT to an increased cancer risk, blood clots, and stroke. This 2002 study lowered HRT supplemetnation, until it's conclusions were challenged and medical consensus developed, placing HRT back as a benefical intervention. Organizations responsible for health governance should be held to high standard of evidential confidence. The effects of mass health misinformation are damaging and pose immediate safety concerns for the consumers of this misinformation.
