## Introduction and Background

There has been large debate within epidemiology and public-health policy surrounding the proper structuring of an "evidence hierarchy." When organizations and governments establish claims and recommendations for public health, it is important that they are relying on strong evidence structures; in contrast to some other fields of research, the promotion of a recommendation on such a large and public scale can be immediately damaging to individual health when that recommendation is harmful, and accidental harmful recommendations often result from relying on weak evidence structures to establish causal claims. There is a long precedent for the reversal of previously confident health and dietary guidance — hormone replacement therapy, beta-carotene, and vitamin E each serve as reference points for this project. The beta-carotene case is especially instructive, as the proposed biological mechanism pointed contrary to the early positive results that were later overturned. Organizations responsible for health governance should be held to a high standard of evidential confidence, because the effects of mass health misinformation are damaging and pose immediate safety concerns for the public that consumes them.

This project does not attempt to determine whether a given recommendation is correct. It asks a narrower and more answerable question: whether the certainty a guidance document asserts for a causal claim matches the evidence structure that actually supports it. Where that certainty outruns its support, the project does not stop at identifying the weakness; it goes on to flesh that gap out by proposing a strong piece of the missing evidence type, one that would run contrary to the claim as stated.

## Theoretical Framework

The evaluative backbone of the project is the Russo-Williamson Thesis (RWT). The RWT calls for a mechanistic explanation alongside difference-making evidence such as RCTs and clinical opinion; a causal claim is established only when there is evidence of both a difference-making relationship between the cause and the effect and a mechanism linking them that is responsible for that difference-making relationship. The thesis is epistemological rather than metaphysical — it concerns the evidence needed to establish causation, and it treats the act of establishment as fallible. Its corollary is evidential pluralism, the position that causal claims rest on multiple and interdependent types of evidence rather than on any single gold standard.

This connects to the concerns raised by the EBM+ movement. Evidence-based medicine relies on the use of "current best evidence," and the EBM+ movement argues that its conventional hierarchy under-weights mechanistic evidence, holding that mechanistic and clinical evidence should be appraised together rather than in isolation. Both legs of the causal-evidence structure carry characteristic failure modes. The issue with a mechanistic approach is fundamentally a lack of knowledge; its failures can come from a false assumption about the workings of a mechanism, an incomplete understanding of a mechanism, or the presence of a counter-mechanism that runs parallel to the original or is set in motion alongside it, rendering an intervention useless or potentially harmful. The issue with causality drawn from clinical trials is the potential presence of an innumerable amount of confounding factors, even within double-blinded, randomized, and placebo-controlled trials; randomized trials can also fail when a large magnitude of exclusion criteria produces an outcome that is beneficial to the population of the study but not applicable or beneficial to the general public. The RWT's demand for both legs is precisely what allows these failures to be located, and it is this diagnostic move — identifying which parts of the causal-evidence structure a recommendation stands on and which it is missing — that the pipeline is built to perform.

## Methodology and Pipeline

The pipeline is at present a structured and partly manual process that is being progressively automated. The stages below describe the general method; for this project specifically, the first two stages are already fixed to a single corpus, as described in the following section — the claims are chosen from the food pyramid and its Scientific Foundation report, and the references and evidence are then drawn from the appendices. It proceeds through the following stages.

1. **Organization and claim selection.** Government or public organizations will be selected to identify public-health claims, drawn from dietary recommendations, foods to implement or restrict, clinical recommendations, advisories, and the like. Selection will follow four criteria: the recommendation's reasoning and evidence should be public and accessible (references to mechanisms, clinical trials, expert opinion); the organization's impact should be widespread and severe; the references must be documentable, organized, and efficient (DOIs, PMIDs, PDFs, downloadable tables); and the recommendations should be firm or confident claims made by the organization. The identification of the specific claims made by a proposal remains a human responsibility.
2. **Reference and sub-reference collection.** The references supporting a proposal and its claims will be collected using an automated system, and the collected data and MD files will be stored and organized in a corpus for documentation and later access. The references of those original references, which we will call sub-references, will likewise be collected and stored. Whether this collection will rely on code alone or on collaboration with an LLM is to be determined.
3. **Evidence hierarchy and chain of authority.** An LLM, using a custom prompt, will analyze the claim and its references to determine the evidence hierarchy of both the claim and the original proposal. It will create the chain of authority and identify which types of evidence the proposal relies on and to what severity — for example clinical trials, mechanistic explanation, meta-analyses, expert opinion, and systematic reviews.
4. **Framework appraisal.** The sub-references will be analyzed in relation to their contribution and application to the references, in order to determine the strength of the references used by the proposal. This analysis will be run by an LLM using existing criteria such as the Cochrane Collection, GRADE, the STROBE Statement, and the Bradford Hill Criteria, with the E-value and quantitative bias analysis used to test robustness against unmeasured confounders. To ensure that the output of the LLM is accurate to the correct application of these criteria, the LLM will be anchored on pre-existing papers demonstrating their correct use, and each framework will be paired with a gold-standard study that illustrates what makes a study pass or fail under that system.
5. **Statistical evaluation.** A statistical system of evaluation will be determined so that it can be applied across the references and sub-references already stored in the corpus. Its exact form is to be determined, and how it should combine with the qualitative framework appraisals rather than sit beside them is one of the open questions below.
6. **Gap identification and constructive challenge.** The different analyses produced by the LLM will be used to determine the strength and validity of the chosen references for the health proposal, and the identification of the evidence hierarchy will be used to show gaps in complete explanation for the proposed causal claims and recommendations. The project does not stop at identifying a weakness in the evidence structure, however. Almost all of the claims chosen for this corpus rest entirely on difference-making evidence, in the form of RCTs and controlled trials, which under the RWT leaves the mechanistic leg of the causal-evidence structure thin or unaddressed. The gap is therefore fleshed out by supplementing that missing leg with existing mechanistic evidence and the anthropological record, drawn specifically so as to run contrary to the proposed claim. This mirrors the beta-carotene case, in which the biological mechanism pointed contrary to the difference-making results that were later overturned; the aim is to move from a diagnosis of what is absent to a concrete presentation of the existing mechanistic and anthropological evidence that, set against the claim, would weigh against the recommendation as it is stated. The evidence supplemented in this way is existing evidence the guidance omitted rather than a hypothetical study, making the challenge a matter of coverage and completeness rather than of proposing new research.

A "finding," in the sense used here, is the construction of a document's evidence hierarchy together with how its foundational papers perform against the E-value, the Cochrane Collection, GRADE, and the evaluation LLM.

## Current Application: The 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines Scientific Foundation Report

For this project specifically, the general pipeline is instantiated on a single fixed corpus rather than harvested from arbitrary organizations. The chosen claims are selected from the food pyramid and its Scientific Foundation report, which serve as the front-end recommendation documents; once those claims are chosen, the references and evidence supporting them are drawn from the appendices, which serve as the back-end evidence corpus. The application is therefore being run against the Scientific Foundation for the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 and its appendices. The work completed so far includes a structural mapping of these documents, in which the main report and its Scientific Foundation carry the GRADE-labeled claims while the appendices house the systematic and umbrella reviews, evidence tables, and ROBIS/GRADE assessments that those claims rest upon. On top of this mapping, an RWT completeness audit has been carried out across the major causal claims, asking which claims carry both difference-making and mechanistic support and which stand on one leg alone; a symmetric check has been applied that holds the document's own stated evidentiary standards back against its own endorsed conclusions; and the selectivity with which Bradford Hill considerations and GRADE upgrades and downgrades are applied has been examined across claims. Provenance signals have also been recorded, including instances of author-evidence overlap, where subject-matter experts rated the certainty of studies they themselves authored, and disclosed financial ties that co-vary with the direction of a recommendation.
