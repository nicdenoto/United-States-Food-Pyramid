## Corpus selection

An organization or agency's public health claims were judged eligible for this pipeline against four criteria: that the reasoning behind a recommendation be public and accessible, referencing mechanisms, clinical trials, or expert opinion rather than asserted without support; that the organization's impact be widespread and severe enough that an error in its recommendations carries real population-level consequence; that its references be documentable, organized, and efficiently retrievable, in the form of DOIs, PMIDs, or downloadable tables rather than informal citation; and that its recommendations be issued as firm, confident claims rather than tentative or exploratory ones, since it is precisely the confidence of a claim that this pipeline is built to interrogate against its evidence structure. The Scientific Foundation for the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030, together with its appendices, satisfies all four criteria at a scale few other single documents do, and was accordingly fixed as this paper's primary corpus rather than treated as one candidate among an open set of organizations. ## The front-end/back-end distinction

The DGA document is best understood as having two layers, and the pipeline is built around auditing both rather than either alone. The front end — the main report — functions as an index of claims and their associated certainty labels; it is the layer a policymaker or member of the public is most likely to read, and the layer at which a recommendation's confidence is actually communicated. The back end — the appendices — contains the evidence structure that is supposed to support those certainty labels: the GRADE ratings, the cited trials and cohort studies, the mechanistic discussion. A recommendation can fail at either layer independently of the other; a front-end certainty label can overstate what its own back-end evidence review supports, and, in at least one case in this corpus, a food group's recommendation can appear in the front end with no dedicated back-end evidence review at all. The pipeline therefore treats claim extraction and evidence-hierarchy analysis as two coordinated but distinct passes over the document, rather than a single read-through.

## Claim and reference extraction

Claims are identified by human review of the front-end recommendations — this step is deliberately not automated, since determining what a document is actually claiming, as opposed to what it merely discusses, still requires judgment that the pipeline does not delegate to a model. Once a claim is identified, its supporting references, and the sub-references of those references in turn, are collected and converted to Markdown for storage in an organized corpus, preserving both the reference itself and enough surrounding context to re-derive how it was used. ## Evidence-hierarchy analysis

With the claim and its reference chain assembled, an LLM is used, via a custom prompt, to analyze the claim and its references and reconstruct the chain of authority behind it: which categories of evidence the claim actually relies upon — clinical trial, mechanistic explanation, expert opinion, systematic review or meta-analysis, observational cohort — and to what severity it relies on each, as opposed to merely gesturing toward a category of evidence without substantively depending on it. The sub-references are analyzed in turn for their contribution to, and adequacy in supporting, the primary references, using the same appraisal apparatus applied to the primary claim: RWT as the governing lens, with GRADE, the Bradford Hill criteria, the E-value, quantitative bias analysis, and, where design-specific evaluation is warranted, ROBIS, AMSTAR-2, RoB 2, ROBINS-I, STROBE, and CONSORT as supporting instruments. Because an LLM's output is only as trustworthy as its calibration against correct human application of these criteria, the model's application of each is checked against worked, gold-standard examples of that criterion's correct use before its judgments on the corpus are relied upon.

## RWT as the operationalizing lens

RWT is not simply one entry in the supporting-apparatus list above; it is the principle that dictates what the evidence-hierarchy analysis is actually checking for. For every claim, the LLM-assisted chain-of-authority reconstruction has to resolve two questions separately, and never conflate them: what difference-making evidence supports the claim, and how strong it remains once adjusted for confounding via quantitative bias analysis, the E-value, and the Bradford Hill criteria; and, independently, what mechanistic evidence supports the claim, and whether that mechanistic account is complete enough to rule out masking — a second, concurrent mechanism producing an effect that counteracts or obscures the first. A claim's asserted certainty is judged proportionate only when both legs are resolved to a level consistent with that certainty, following RWT's requirement that a causal claim needs both legs, not either in isolation; where one leg is missing, weak, or has been assumed rather than established, the claim is flagged as under-supported regardless of how strong the other leg happens to be. This is what turns RWT from a citation in the literature review into an operating rule of the pipeline itself: the whole-grains case, where the RCT record supports mechanism more convincingly than it supports difference-making, and the saturated-fat case, positioned as a reversal performed in real time rather than retrospectively, are both direct applications of this two-leg check, not illustrations appended to the methodology after the fact.

## LLM calibration: justification, parameters, replicability

Because the evidence-hierarchy analysis and the RWT dual-leg check above are performed with LLM assistance, the pipeline's credibility depends on being able to state, and defend, how the model was calibrated before its outputs on the DGA corpus were trusted. This is documented under three headings.

**Justification.** [DRAFT — grounded in an observation already recorded for this project; confirm the framing before treating it as final.] The claims this pipeline asks the LLM to adjudicate do not admit the kind of mechanical check a code output does, where an incorrect result visibly fails; an incorrect evidentiary judgment instead reads as a plausible one. An earlier phase of this project, working with LLM-assisted code generation, saw correction rates decline across successive research-log entries; that decline reflects the shift from a verifiable task, where a wrong output announces itself, to an unverifiable one, where it does not — not necessarily an improvement in the model's underlying reasoning. This is precisely the failure mode calibration is built to guard against: before the model's chain-of-authority reconstructions, GRADE ratings, or RWT assessments on the DGA corpus are relied upon, its outputs on a fixed set of worked examples with a known-correct human application of the relevant criterion are checked against that known answer, so that confidence in the model's judgment rests on demonstrated agreement rather than on the fluency of its prose.

**Parameters.** [T/BD — not yet fixed in the material available; the shape below is what needs to be settled and locked before this subsection is final.] The number and source of gold-standard worked examples used per criterion (RWT, GRADE, Bradford Hill, the E-value, QBA, and any design-specific instrument such as ROBIS or STROBE actually invoked on this corpus); whether those examples are drawn from published methodological papers, from worked analyses produced by Nic and supervisors, or both; the exact prompt template used for calibration versus for live corpus analysis, and whether the two are the same instrument; the model and model version in use; and the agreement threshold — exact match, rubric-scored, or supervisor-adjudicated — that the model must clear on the calibration set before its output on an actual DGA claim is accepted rather than routed to human review.

**Replicability.** For a pipeline making causal-evidentiary judgments under faculty supervision, replicability is part of the argument that its outputs can be trusted at all, not a courtesy appended afterward. This requires versioning and preserving, alongside the corpus itself: the calibration example set, so a later reviewer can see exactly what the model was checked against; the exact prompts used at each pipeline stage, since a rephrased prompt is a different, unvalidated instrument even when the underlying criterion is unchanged; the model identifier and version, since calibration performed against one version does not transfer to another; and a research log of every human correction made during calibration or live analysis, capturing not just that a correction occurred but what the model got wrong and why. The Week 2–3 AI-correction transcripts already flagged elsewhere in this project as high-priority evidentiary material are exactly this record, and belong here as the documented basis for this replicability requirement.

## Provenance discipline and the burden-of-proof check

Every claim carried through the pipeline is tagged at one of three provenance tiers: verified directly against a primary document, sourced from a secondary reference or search and not yet corpus-verified, or inferred from the structure of the argument rather than stated outright. Conflating these tiers is treated as a methodological error in its own right, independent of whether the underlying claim later turns out to be correct. This discipline supports the pipeline's central analytical move, which is to apply the report's own evidentiary standard symmetrically: the DGA appendices apply a directional standard of evidence when supporting their preferred recommendations, and this pipeline applies that same standard back to those recommendations rather than granting them a presumption the appendices do not extend to competing evidence.

## From diagnosis to construction

Where the evidence-hierarchy analysis identifies a recommendation whose asserted certainty outruns its evidence structure — an unaddressed reliance on a single evidential leg, or a mechanism acknowledged elsewhere in the document but never applied back to the recommendation in question — the pipeline's aim is not to stop at naming the gap. Existing contrary evidence, mechanistic or anthropological, is deliberately sought out and supplied as a substitute for the missing leg, so that each case analysis in this paper ends with a proposed evidentiary correction rather than only a critique.
